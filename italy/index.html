Getting there & home

Out: Thursday night Oct 22. Flight departs JFK late/overnight — be at the terminal by 9 PM. Leave Binghamton by 3:30 PM to allow 3+ hours travel plus check-in buffer. Home: everyone flies out of Venice Nov 4 — details at the bottom.

1 Private Car Service — recommended ~$300–400 total · door-to-terminal ▼

Best choice for 3 people with luggage. A private SUV picks you up at your Binghamton door and drops all three of you at your departure terminal — no transfers, no dragging bags through Port Authority.

Contact Binghamton Limousine or Winfield Transportation — both serve BGM→JFK. At ~$350 total, split three ways it's about $115/person. Send this draft:

2 Greyhound or FlixBus — the real daily options ~$38–48/person · 3.5 hrs ▼

I checked which buses actually run Binghamton→NYC in 2026. Greyhound and FlixBus are the two that run every day (same company now, same terminal). Both leave from the Greater Binghamton Transportation Center, 81 Chenango St.

  • Greyhound — ~15 departures/day, ~3h25, ~$39. Arrives Port Authority (42nd St). Target a 2–4 PM departure to land by ~7 PM.
  • FlixBus — daily, ~3.5h, ~$38–48. Arrives Hudson Yards (W 34th St).
Skip these for Oct 22: OurBus is now weekend-only (BU students) — unreliable on a Thursday. Trailways runs Fri/Sun only. Coach USA/ShortLine status is uncertain after bankruptcy. Greyhound/FlixBus are the safe daily bets — but still confirm the exact Oct 22 clock times when booking.

Then bus → JFK: with 3 people + bags heading to a late flight, take a taxi/rideshare (~$70 flat from Manhattan, 45–90 min) rather than wrangling the AirTrain. The private car in Option 1 still saves ~$150 total and skips two bag transfers.

Drive to JFK and use long-term parking (~$25/day × 13 days = ~$325). You avoid any ground transfer at both ends, but you're driving into JFK traffic right before an international flight and paying the same as a private car. Fine if you prefer not coordinating with a driver.

Day-by-day Rome

Arriving at Fiumicino (FCO): with 3 people + bags, take the official white taxi — flat €55 per car to anywhere inside the old city walls, luggage included (cheaper than 3× Leonardo Express tickets at €14 each). Follow the official taxi-rank signs; ignore anyone approaching you inside the terminal.
Two date quirks this week: (1) Oct 25 is a Sunday — Vatican Museums are closed, and several top restaurants (Roscioli, Da Enzo, Armando) don't open Sundays. The plan below routes around it. (2) Clocks fall back the night of Oct 24→25 — free extra hour of sleep, but sunset drops to ~5:10 PM after that, so do outdoor sights before 5.

Tuscany

Arrive Oct 27 · 4 nights · Chianti hills, Val d'Orcia & the hot springs

Arrive Tue Oct 27 from Rome. All three of you in the Chianti hills: vineyards outside the window, wine at dinner every night, no grocery shopping ever. This is where the two things you loved most live — the Val d'Orcia UNESCO hills and the Saturnia hot springs — so it earns its 4 nights. Florence is the next stop, one hour up the road (see Along the Way).

ZTL parking rule: Historic hill towns (Siena, San Gimignano, Cortona) have camera-enforced restricted zones. Drive to the edge, park FREE at the signposted parcheggio outside the walls (5-min walk max), walk in. Never drive into the stone-walled centers. Your agriturismo always has free parking — no ZTL concerns at home base.
SS222 — The Chiantigiana: The road from Florence south through Greve in Chianti to Siena. If you do nothing else in Tuscany, drive this road. Rolling vineyards, medieval farmhouses, cypress-lined lanes, Chianti black rooster signs at every gate. This is the postcard. Take it slow on the drive in and again on the wine day.

Day-by-day Tuscany

You're landing in olive-harvest week. Late October is the raccolta — the presses (frantoi) run day and night and olio nuovo, the peppery bright-green new oil, hits the bruschetta everywhere. Wine cellars are mid-fermentation (the best tour timing of the year), porcini and the first truffles are on every menu, and the vineyards turn gold. This is arguably the best eating week of the Tuscan calendar. Ask your agriturismo if you can watch (or join) the olive picking.
Tue Oct 27 · Arrive from Rome (dark)

You'll roll in from Rome around 6:45 PM — it's dark by 5:10 now, so skip sightseeing and go straight to the farm. Drop bags, glass of house wine, eat whatever they're serving. That's it. That's the plan. (Save Greve's piazza and Enoteca Falorni — the oldest wine shop in Chianti, pouring since 1806 — for tomorrow in daylight.)

Fri Oct 30 onward · Saturnia hot springs (or the flexible tail)

Cascate del Mulino — steaming waterfalls on Halloween

Hidden gem · free entry

The most unique thing in Tuscany: naturally volcanic 37.5°C water cascading over terraced travertine pools in open countryside. Free, open 24/7, and in late October the pools are near-empty while the air is crisp — the steam rising off the waterfalls is unforgettable.

Honest logistics (updated): it's a real drive — ~2.5–2.75 hrs each way from Chianti (via Siena → E78 → Scansano), not the "90 minutes" older blogs claim. And parking changed: there's now a paid regulated lot ~200 m from the falls (~€2.50/hr) with toilets, showers and €5 lockers — don't park outside the designated areas (fines/towing). Bring towels, robes, water shoes (the travertine is rough) and a dry change of clothes; it's chilly getting out.
  • 6:45 AMLeave the farm with coffee + cornetti to go. Sunrise drive south through the Val d'Orcia edges.
  • 9:30 AMPools. Soak 2–3 hours. The waterfall pools at the old mill are the postcard spot; the upper terraces are warmer and quieter.
  • 12:30 PMLunch 10 min away in Montemerano (one of Italy's "borghi più belli" — tiny medieval perfection) — or grab porchetta panini in Saturnia village.
  • 2:00 PMDrive home (~2.5 hrs). Nap when you arrive.
  • 7:30 PMLast farm dinner. Toast with the estate's olio nuovo on everything.

Mellow alternative if a 5-hour round-trip drive sounds wrong after the wine day: skip Saturnia, sleep in, walk to the hamlet of Volpaia or Montefioralle for lunch, watch the olive pressing at the farm, and have a 20-minute drive kind of day. Both versions are right; decide the night before based on energy.

Venice

Quick and early — the magic without the human zoo

🚤 The plan: keep Venice light

After Mom flies home, it's just the two of you, and you don't want the cruise-ship crush. So Venice is a quick visit, not a 3-night stay — arrive the evening before or early morning, see the canals and the quiet corners while day-trippers are still on their trains, then take the direct FlixBus to Ljubljana (~4 hrs). You get the beauty and skip the zoo.

Two ways to do it: (1) One night — sleep in Venice, get the magical empty-at-dawn morning, bus out midday. (2) Pure day-trip — come in early from Padua/Verona, spend the day, bus to Slovenia that evening. Either keeps it short and sweet.

Early November is already the quietest season — mist on the water, gold light, locals outnumbering tourists.

Acqua alta — know before you go

November is high-tide season, but the MOSE barriers (operational since 2020) now stop the catastrophic floods — they rise whenever forecasts exceed ~110 cm. What MOSE does not stop: moderate tides of 85–100 cm that put a few hours of ankle-deep water in Piazza San Marco (the lowest point in the city) on some mornings.

  • Download the Hi!Tide Venice app for tide forecasts — sirens also sound ~3 hrs ahead.
  • The city lays out raised walkways (passerelle) on main routes automatically.
  • If a tide catches you, kiosks sell knee-high shoe covers for €10–15. Waterproof shoes + warm socks cover 99% of scenarios — no rubber boots needed.

November is still mild (16–18°C). Eixample or Gràcia neighborhoods have excellent Airbnbs and cafés with good WiFi. Tapas scene is arguably the best in Europe. Direct flight from Venice. The architecture (Gaudí) is worth a week just for that.
