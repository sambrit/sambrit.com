Binghamton → JFK

Thursday night Oct 22. Flight departs JFK late/overnight — be at the terminal by 9 PM. Leave Binghamton by 3:30 PM to allow 3+ hours travel time plus check-in buffer.

Best choice for 3 people with luggage. A private SUV picks you up at your Binghamton door and drops all three of you at your departure terminal — no transfers, no dragging bags through Port Authority.

2 Trailways / Greyhound Bus ~$30–50/person · Port Authority NYC ▼

Several express coaches daily from Binghamton to Port Authority Bus Terminal in Midtown Manhattan. Target a 3 PM or 4 PM departure to arrive NYC by 7 PM, then 45–60 min taxi to JFK (recommended with luggage) or AirTrain+subway ($9).

Check current schedules: Book directly at trailways.com or greyhound.com — schedules change seasonally. For 3 people with rolling bags, the private car option saves about $150 total and eliminates 2 bag transfers.

Drive to JFK and use long-term parking (~$25/day × 13 days = ~$325). You avoid any ground transfer at both ends, but you're driving into JFK traffic right before an international flight and paying the same as a private car. Fine if you prefer not coordinating with a driver.

Day-by-day Rome

Day 1 — Friday Oct 23 · Arrive

This is a recovery day. Land, collect bags, get to the Airbnb, nap. Do not fight the jet lag — you've been up all night on a transatlantic flight.

Late afternoon: Force yourself awake by 4 PM. Walk to the nearest espresso bar. If near Trastevere, wander to Piazza di Santa Maria in Trastevere — one of Rome's most beautiful squares. The fountain, the basilica façade, the aperitivo crowd.

Dinner: Da Enzo al 29 — the quintessential Roman trattoria in Trastevere. Cacio e pepe, coda alla vaccinara (braised oxtail), abbacchio (roast lamb). Reserve on their website 2–3 weeks ahead.

Da Enzo al 29
Day 2 — Saturday Oct 24 · Vatican + Borghese (2 PM)

Vatican Museums + Borghese Gallery

Borghese: ✓ Booked

Morning 8:30–9 AM: Vatican Museums + Sistine Chapel. This takes 3–4 hours. Go with a skip-the-line guided small-group tour — walk-in lines regularly exceed 2 hours, and a guide gets you through the galleries faster. Book at getyourguide.com or the official Vatican site (museivaticani.va).

Quick lunch near the Vatican (Prati neighborhood). Then taxi or short walk to Villa Borghese park.

2:00 PM sharp: Borghese Gallery — your confirmed slot. Be at the gate 15 minutes early. This is 2 hours of Bernini sculptures, Caravaggio paintings, and Raphael — arguably the finest art collection in any building in the world.

Evening: Aperitivo at Freni e Frizioni in Trastevere. Dinner: Glass Hostaria (Michelin-starred, Trastevere — book now).

Vatican Museums (official) GetYourGuide (skip-the-line tours)
Day 3 — Sunday Oct 25 · Colosseum

Colosseum + Roman Forum + Palatine Hill

Book ahead — coopculture.it

Buy the combo ticket including Forum and Palatine (€16 + €2 booking). Pre-book the Underground Arena add-on to walk where gladiators waited before entering the ring — not accessible on general admission. Plan 3–4 hours here.

Walk through the Forum afterwards: the Via Sacra, the Arch of Titus, the Temple of Saturn. Then up to Palatine Hill for panoramic views over Rome.

Dinner: Roscioli (deli-restaurant hybrid in the old ghetto area — extraordinary pastas, cured meats and wine list. Book 2–3 weeks ahead) or Emma Pizzeria (Neapolitan pizza near the Pantheon).

Book Colosseum (coopculture.it)
Day 4 — Monday Oct 26 · Neighborhoods + Spa

Testaccio Market (Rome's best food market — suppli, mortadella sandwiches, fresh pasta, closes at 2 PM). Then Pantheon (free, but long line — arrive at 9 AM opening or book the €5 timed entry). Piazza Navona for people-watching.

Afternoon: QC Terme day spa or hotel spa if you've upgraded. Book in advance. A massage, Roman baths, and mineral pools — exactly what you wanted.

Dinner: Osteria dell'Angelo — fixed-price Roman feast in Prati (near the Vatican). No menu decisions, just wave after wave of classic Roman plates. Legendary for a reason.

Day 5 — Tuesday Oct 27 · Free + Wander

Sleep in. No agenda. Walk wherever looks interesting. Trevi Fountain is best before 8 AM or after 11 PM — go at one of those times. Campo de' Fiori has a good produce market in the morning.

Worth adding: Palazzo Doria Pamphilj — a private palace museum that most tourists skip. Contains one of Rome's finest art collections (Velázquez, Titian, Caravaggio) in a working family palace. Usually no wait at all.

Dinner: Tonnarello (Trastevere, buzzy atmosphere, classics done well) or cook at the Airbnb with stuff from the morning market.

Tuscany — 4 nights

Oct 28 – Nov 1. All three of you in the Chianti hills. Vineyards outside the window, wine at dinner every night, no grocery shopping ever.

ZTL parking rule: Historic hill towns (Siena, San Gimignano, Cortona) have camera-enforced restricted zones. Drive to the edge, park FREE at the signposted parcheggio outside the walls (5-min walk max), walk in. Never drive into the stone-walled centers. Your agriturismo always has free parking — no ZTL concerns at home base.
SS222 — The Chiantigiana: The road from Florence south through Greve in Chianti to Siena. If you do nothing else in Tuscany, drive this road. Rolling vineyards, medieval farmhouses, cypress-lined lanes, Chianti black rooster signs at every gate. This is the postcard. Take it slow on Day 6 when you arrive.

Day-by-day Tuscany

Day 6 — Oct 28 · Arrive via pasta class (see Rome)

After your 1 PM pasta class in Rome, pick up the rental car near Termini (~3:30 PM) and drive north on A1, then exit to the SS222 Chiantigiana through the heart of Chianti wine country. Pull over and stop in Greve in Chianti for a quick look around the main piazza and maybe a wine shop tasting (Enoteca Falorni — the oldest wine shop in Chianti, in the piazza since 1806).

Arrive agriturismo by 6:30–7 PM. You'll be tired from Rome. Drop bags, have a glass of the house wine, eat whatever the farm is serving. That's it. That's the plan.

Day 7 — Oct 29 · Siena

Drive 30–45 min to Siena. Park at Parcheggio Il Campo or Stadio Comunale outside the walls. Walk up through the medieval gate into the city.

Piazza del Campo — the shell-shaped piazza is one of the greatest public spaces in Europe. Sit at a café and watch it for a while. Duomo di Siena — grander than most cathedrals, floor covered in elaborate marble inlaid designs. The Libreria Piccolomini inside has stunning Renaissance frescoes. Book tickets at operaduomo.siena.it.

Lunch on the Campo (splurge). Back to the agriturismo by late afternoon. Farm dinner and Chianti.

Siena Duomo tickets
Day 8 — Oct 30 · Vineyard crawl + Chianti roads

This is the wine day. Book winery visits in advance — not just the gift shop, a proper cellar tour and seated tasting.

  • Castello di Brolio (Barone Ricasoli) — the estate that literally invented the modern Chianti blend in 1872. Stunning medieval castle, full cellar tours, panoramic views. Book at ricasoli.com.
  • Antinori nel Chianti Classico — spectacular modern winery architecture built into a hillside underground. Tours of the circular cave cellar are remarkable. The Osteria on-site has excellent food too.
  • Or: stay small with Fèlsina or Isole e Olena for a more personal, intimate tasting.

Drive the Chiantigiana between stops. This road is the point. Budget 1.5 hours per winery.

Day 9 — Oct 31 · Saturnia Hot Springs (hidden gem) + San Gimignano

Saturnia Thermal Springs — Italy's best-kept secret

Hidden gem

This is the most unique thing in Tuscany. Saturnia is a series of naturally occurring volcanic hot spring pools in the countryside near Grosseto — the water runs at 37°C (99°F) and cascades in terraced waterfalls surrounded by fields. It's completely free, open 24 hours, and most tourists don't know it exists. On Halloween morning with steam rising off the hot water — unforgettable.

Logistics: Drive south from Chianti (~1.5–2 hrs). Arrive early (6–7 AM is magical — sunrise over steaming waterfalls). Bring towels and a change of clothes. Park free in the gravel lot. The famous "Cascate del Mulino" waterfall section is right at the parking lot.

On the way back: Stop at San Gimignano (~1 hr from Saturnia) — the medieval towers, the piazza, and Gelateria Dondoli (two-time World Gelato Championship winners — the saffron gelato is extraordinary).

Home to the agriturismo for the last farm dinner.

Where to stay — 3 agriturismo options (€150–300/night)

All three are working farms. On-site meals, farm wine, breakfast included. No grocery shopping. Dinner is at the farm. Free parking.

1 Fattoria di Rignana — Greve in Chianti €102–168/night · Pool + vineyard · verified ▼

Best value + atmosphere for your group. A 120-hectare working estate with vineyards, olive groves, and an outdoor pool. They grow and make almost everything you eat and drink here — wine, olive oil, vegetables. The farmhouse rooms are simple but beautifully characterful. Breakfast included. Dinner available on-site (the farm restaurant is genuinely good).

Location: Greve in Chianti — right in the heart of the Classico zone. 25 min to Siena, 30 min to Florence. Note: there's an unpaved access road for the last stretch — totally normal for Chianti farmhouses, just take it slow.

Price: €102–168/night double room. For 3 adults you'll need 2 rooms — budget ~€200–336/night total. Well within your range.

Fattoria di Rignana
2 Castello di Verrazzano — Greve in Chianti €145–250/night · Medieval castle, km-zero meals ▼

A genuine medieval castle (same Verrazzano as the NYC bridge — the explorer was born here in 1485) with accommodation in the historic foresteria (guest quarters). Vineyard setting, pergola dining terrace, breakfast from their own farm ("km zero" products — olive oil, honey, wine, vegetables). 20 km from Florence, easy day-trip access.

No pool, but the terraced gardens and vineyard views are stunning. They do wine tours and tastings of their own Chianti Classico on-site.

Castello di Verrazzano
3 Rosewood Castiglion del Bosco — Montalcino ~$600–900/night · Ultra-luxury, Brunello country ▼

If budget isn't the constraint, this is one of the finest properties in Italy — a 5,000-acre private estate near Montalcino (home of Brunello di Montalcino, arguably Italy's greatest red wine). Private vineyard, two restaurants, spa, 39 restored stone farmhouses. A 30-minute detour from Chianti but a completely different level of experience. Worth it for a special occasion.

Venice — 3 nights

Nov 1–4. Early November in Venice is one of Italy's best-kept secrets: nearly no tourists, mist rising off the lagoon, golden light, none of the summer crush. No cars anywhere — your parking anxiety is completely gone.

Florence → Venice: Return the rental car at Florence airport (Aeroporto Peretola, FLR — 25 min from Chianti). Take the Frecciarossa or Italo high-speed train from Firenze SMN → Venezia Santa Lucia. ~2 hours, ~€30–50/person. Trains every hour. Book a few days ahead for the best prices at trenitalia.com or thetrainline.com.
Day 10 — Nov 1 · Arrive + Grand Canal

Check in. Walk the Rialto Bridge and the Rialto Market area (it'll be late afternoon — just get oriented). Take vaporetto Line 1 down the Grand Canal from Santa Lucia station to San Marco — €9.50, slowest water bus, most spectacular view. The palazzo facades reflected in the canal at dusk.

Dinner: Osteria alle Testiere — 22 seats, the best restaurant in Venice. Lagoon cuttlefish in ink, spider crab pasta, Soave. Book 3–4 weeks ahead. The chef Marco shops at the Rialto fish market personally every morning.

Alle Testiere (reserve now) Trenitalia Florence→Venice
Day 11 — Nov 2 · St. Mark's + Doge's Palace + getting lost

Morning: Basilica di San Marco — free entry but book the timed reservation at veneziaunica.it (€3 to skip the queue). Go early. The Byzantine mosaics covering every inch of the ceiling and the four bronze horses are astonishing.

Doge's Palace: Book the Secret Itineraries Tour — this takes you through rooms and passageways not on the general route, including the cell where Casanova was imprisoned and from which he made his famous escape. Available at palazzoducale.visitmuve.it.

Afternoon: Get deliberately lost. Walk away from San Marco toward Dorsoduro or Cannaregio and just wander. At aperitivo hour (6 PM), stop at whichever bar has the most locals and order a Spritz and cicchetti — little crostini with seafood, €1–2 each. This is how Venetians eat before dinner.

Dinner: Antiche Carampane (no pizza on the menu — that's a deliberate policy and a good sign; outstanding seafood in a residential neighborhood almost no tourist finds).

Day 12 — Nov 3 · Murano + Burano outer islands

Take the vaporetto to the outer lagoon islands. Murano: the world capital of hand-blown glass since 1291. Watch a master glassblower work at a real furnace (not the tourist shops — find a working fornace with a demo). Burano: every building painted a different bright color by law — fishermen's tradition so they could see their house from the water. In November mist, with no crowds, it's extraordinarily beautiful.

Last Venice dinner: Venissa on Mazzorbo island (between Murano and Burano) — a Michelin-starred restaurant on a small island with its own vineyard. The lagoon view at dinner is the perfect way to end Italy.

Venice hotels — 3 options

1 Palazzo Venart Luxury Hotel ~€400–600/night · Rooftop terrace + Grand Canal ▼

A 16th-century palazzo on the Grand Canal converted into a 18-room luxury hotel. Stunning rooftop terrace with 360° lagoon views, private garden, and the on-site restaurant Glam (Michelin-starred). Intimate enough to feel like a private palazzo, professional enough to have real hotel service. This is the sweet spot between characterful and comfortable.

Palazzo Venart
2 Hotel Nani Mocenigo Palace ~€200–350/night · Dorsoduro, local neighborhood ▼

A 15th-century palazzo in Dorsoduro — Venice's most livable neighborhood (university area, local restaurants, the Zattere promenade along the Giudecca canal). €200–350/night, canal views, genuine Venetian character without the San Marco tourist bubble. Three of you can be very comfortable here.

3 The Gritti Palace (Marriott Luxury Collection) ~$800–1,400/night · Grand Canal, legendary ▼

If you're splurging for the finale, The Gritti is the grande dame of Venice — a 15th-century palazzo directly on the Grand Canal, Ernest Hemingway and Truman Capote's former haunt, full spa, terrace dining over the water. This is expensive but it is one of the great hotel experiences on earth.

The Gritti Palace

After Italy: Sam + Siri remote work week

1 Amsterdam ~$1,500–2,500/week · Best for remote work ▼

Direct flights from Venice Marco Polo. English everywhere, excellent fast WiFi, canal apartment Airbnbs in the Jordaan or De Pijp neighborhoods for $200–350/night. World-class food scene (Indonesian, French, Dutch). November is cool but walkable and uncrowded. The coffee shop scene is a bonus.

2 Lisbon ~$1,200–2,000/week · Best weather + value ▼

Most affordable of the three. 18–20°C in early November. Excellent Airbnbs in Bairro Alto or Mouraria, €150–250/night. Strong digital nomad infrastructure. Restaurants are outstanding and cheap. Pastéis de nata for breakfast every morning.

3 Barcelona ~$1,500–2,500/week · Best food + architecture ▼

November is still mild (16–18°C). Eixample or Gràcia neighborhoods have excellent Airbnbs and cafés with good WiFi. Tapas scene is arguably the best in Europe. Direct flight from Venice. The architecture (Gaudí) is worth a week just for that.
