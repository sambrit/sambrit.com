Binghamton → JFK

Night of Thursday, Oct 22. Your flight departs late/overnight — you need to be at JFK by ~9 PM. Leave Binghamton by 3–5 PM to give yourself buffer (the ride is ~3–3.5 hours without traffic, then taxi/AirTrain to terminal).

1 Private Car Service — Most Comfortable ~$300–400 · door-to-terminal ▼

Best option for 3 people with luggage. A black-car or private SUV picks you up at the Binghamton door and drops all three of you at the JFK departure terminal — no transfers, no bus stations, no taxi scramble at Port Authority with bags.

Who to contact: Reach out to Binghamton Limousine & Transportation or Winfield Transportation — both serve BGM→JFK. At ~$350 split three ways it's under $120/person and vastly less stressful than the bus.

2 Trailways Bus — Budget Pick ~$30–50 per person · Port Authority ▼

Trailways of New York runs express coaches from Binghamton Chenango St to Port Authority Bus Terminal (NYC) several times per day, roughly every 4 hours. For an Oct 22 evening departure you want a 3 PM or 5 PM bus — arriving Port Authority ~6:30–8:30 PM, then 60 min taxi or AirTrain to JFK.

Check live times: Schedules change seasonally. Book at trailways.com/schedules — sort by Oct 22 departures from Binghamton. For 3 people with rolling bags the $30/seat saving vs. private car is small, and you'll handle luggage on/off the bus + at Port Authority.

Greyhound alternative: 5 departures/day, also to Port Authority. FlixBus goes to Midtown (3x/day). Both bookable at their sites or on Google Maps transit.

3 Rental Car + Park at JFK — DIY ~$80–120 · most flexible ▼

Rent a one-way car from Binghamton, drive to JFK (~3 hrs), return the car at JFK's rental center. Long-term JFK parking is also an option (~$25/day x 12 days = $300) if you'd rather drive your own car and not deal with rental logistics.

Note: This adds a drop-off errand right before an international flight. Generally the private car option is less stress unless cost is the primary concern.

JFK to the gate

Most trans-Atlantic flights depart from Terminal 1, 4, or 7. Check your airline. Once at Port Authority (if taking bus), grab a taxi (~$70 flat rate) or the AirTrain + E/J/Z subway ($7) — taxi is strongly preferred with luggage for 3.

Day-by-Day in Rome

Day 1 — Friday Oct 23 · Arrive + Recover

Morning/afternoon: Land, collect bags, get to Airbnb. Nap is mandatory — you've been up all night. This is not a sightseeing day.

Late afternoon: Walk to the nearest neighborhood bar for espresso (do not fall asleep at 4 PM — push through until 9 PM). If your Airbnb is near Trastevere, wander the streets: Piazza di Santa Maria in Trastevere, the fountains, the market stalls.

Dinner: Da Enzo al 29 — classic Roman trattoria, Trastevere. Cacio e pepe, coda alla vaccinara (oxtail). Reservations online 3–4 weeks ahead.

Day 2 — Saturday Oct 24 · Vatican + Borghese

The Vatican

Book morning slot

Morning 8 AM: Vatican Museums + Sistine Chapel. Go early via skip-the-line tour to beat the crowd. The line for walk-ins routinely reaches 2+ hours and you can't enter the Sistine Chapel without getting through the museums first.

Late morning: St. Peter's Basilica — free entry, no ticket needed. Climb the dome (~€8) for views over Rome. Budget 2 hours.

GetYourGuide Skip-the-line (~€33) Official Vatican Tickets (€18 + booking fee)

Borghese Gallery

Confirmed · 2:00–4:00 PM

Afternoon 2:00 PM: Borghese Gallery — the world's finest private art collection (Bernini, Caravaggio, Raphael) in a villa inside Villa Borghese park. Seats 244/245/246 · 3 tickets · €54 paid. Strict timed entry, 2-hour visit. Arrive 15 min early.

Dinner: Glass Hostaria (Michelin-starred, Trastevere, book 4+ weeks ahead) — or Freni e Frizioni for excellent aperitivo cocktails first, then dinner nearby.

Day 3 — Sunday Oct 25 · Colosseum

Colosseum + Roman Forum + Palatine Hill

Book underground access

Morning/Afternoon: Buy combo ticket at coopculture.it. Book a small-group guided tour to get the underground arena access (normally off-limits). Budget 3–4 hours.

Dinner: Campo de' Fiori area. Try Roscioli (outstanding deli/restaurant, book in advance) or Emma Pizzeria for Neapolitan wood-fired pizza near the Pantheon.

Book Colosseum
Day 4 — Monday Oct 26 · Day Trip to Orvieto

Orvieto — Hidden Gem, 90 min from Rome

Off the beaten path

A medieval cliff-top city in Umbria, dramatically perched on volcanic rock, best seen as a day trip from Rome. Far fewer tourists than Florence. Highlights: the Duomo di Orvieto (better exterior than Notre Dame, some say), the Etruscan underground caves (book tour at orvietounderground.it), Pozzo di San Patrizio (a double-helix Renaissance well), local Orvieto Classico white wine.

Getting there: Train from Roma Termini → Orvieto station (~1.5 hrs, €12–20). Funicular from the station up to the old city (€1.30 each way). This also puts you en route to Tuscany if you want to drive or stop.

Dinner back in Rome: Le Mani in Pasta (Trastevere, upscale pasta/seafood, reservations recommended) or back to your Airbnb neighborhood bar.

Day 5 — Tuesday Oct 27 · Free Day / Neighborhoods

Slow morning. Testaccio Market (Rome's best food market, closes at 2 PM) — eat suppli (fried rice balls), fresh pasta, local cheese. Then walk to Piazza Navona, the Pantheon (free, long lines — go early), Trevi Fountain (go at 7 AM or after midnight to avoid crowds).

Afternoon spa: If staying at Villa Agrippina, use the Clarins spa — book a treatment (massage) in advance at the hotel.

Dinner: Osteria dell'Angelo (Prati neighborhood, fixed-price Roman feast, legendary) or Tonnarello (Trastevere, buzzing atmosphere, classic plates).

Where to stay in Rome — 3 options

1 Villa Agrippina Gran Meliá ~$350–500/night · Outdoor pool + Clarins spa ▼

The clear winner for what you want. Historic 17th-century villa turned 5-star hotel, 800 meters from the Vatican, walkable to Trastevere. Stunning sunset views from the garden. The rooms overlook the Tiber or the gardens.

Villa Agrippina sits just outside the ZTL zone — no driving restrictions when you arrive. When you're ready to drive to Tuscany on Oct 28, just pick up the rental car at a nearby agency.

Tuscany — 4–5 nights

Oct 28 – Nov 1 (drive or train from Rome). All three of you together in the Chianti hills. Vineyards, wine, hill towns, Siena, no grocery shopping ever.

ZTL Parking rule for Tuscany: Historic centers (Siena, San Gimignano, Cortona) have Zona Traffico Limitato zones — cameras will ticket you automatically if you drive in. The move: drive to the edge of the old town, park FREE at the signposted parking areas outside the walls (usually within 5 min walk), and walk in. Never drive into the stone-walled city centers. Your agriturismo will always have free parking — no ZTL issues at the property.

Day-by-Day in Tuscany

Day 6 — Wednesday Oct 28 · Pasta Class + Drive to Chianti

1:00 PM — Pasta-making class near Piazza Navona (~2 hours, ~€50–65/person). Search Viator "pasta Piazza Navona 1pm" to book. Learn to make fresh pasta from scratch in the heart of Rome before heading to Tuscany.

~3:30 PM — Pick up rental car near Roma Termini (book auto-transmission; Hertz, Europcar, or Sixt). Drive north on the A1 autostrada, then scenic SS222 (Chiantigiana road) through the heart of Chianti wine country. ~2.5–3 hrs direct.

Stop en route: Greve in Chianti (the unofficial capital of Chianti wine country) — browse Enoteca Falorni, the oldest wine shop in Chianti, for a tasting.

Arrive agriturismo by 7–8 PM. Pre-dinner aperitivo on the terrace (farm wine included). Dinner on-site.

Day 7 — Wednesday Oct 29 · Siena

Drive to Siena (~30–45 min from Chianti). Park at Parcheggio Il Campo or Parcheggio Stadio Comunale outside the walls (~€2–3/hr). Walk up into the city. Piazza del Campo (one of Europe's great medieval piazzas), Duomo di Siena (book tickets at operaduomo.siena.it to access the full complex), the Libreria Piccolomini, terracotta rooftops.

Lunch at a trattoria on Piazza del Campo. Back to the agriturismo by late afternoon. Wine tasting with the owner in the evening.

Day 8 — Thursday Oct 30 · Vineyard day

This is the wine day. The Chianti Classico DOCG zone is right outside your door. Visit 2–3 wineries. Worth booking in advance for a proper cellar tour + tasting (not just the gift shop):

  • Castello di Brolio (Barone Ricasoli — the estate that invented Chianti; epic castle, full cellar tours)
  • Antinori nel Chianti Classico (modern architecture, underground cellar carved into the hill — spectacular)
  • Fonterutoli or Fèlsina for smaller, more intimate tasting rooms

Don't drive after a full day of tastings — designate a driver or do one winery seriously and come home for the farm dinner.

Day 9 — Friday Oct 31 · Saturnia Hot Springs + San Gimignano

Cascate del Mulino — Saturnia Hot Springs

Hidden gem · Free entry

Morning: Drive to Saturnia hot springs (~2 hrs from Chianti). The Cascate del Mulino are free, natural 37°C volcanic pools cascading down travertine terraces. Open 24/7, no tickets needed. Go early morning to beat crowds. Bring towels and water shoes. Halloween in a volcanic hot spring — hard to top.

Afternoon (on the way back): Stop at San Gimignano (medieval towers, gelato di San Gimignano — Gelateria Dondoli has won the World Gelato Championship multiple times). Park outside the walls at Parcheggio Montemaggio.

Evening: last farm dinner and wine at the agriturismo.

Day 10 — Saturday Nov 1 · Drive to Venice

Return rental car at Florence airport (Aeroporto di Firenze–Peretola, 30 min from Chianti). Take the Frecciarossa or Italo high-speed train Florence SMN → Venezia Santa Lucia. ~2 hours, ~€30–60/person. Book at trenitalia.com or thetrainline.com. 25+ trains per day — no need to pre-book far ahead but do book a few days before to lock the price. Drop bags at hotel, walk to the Grand Canal. Venice in the evening light in early November (off-season) is extraordinary.

Where to stay in Tuscany — 3 options

All three are agriturismi — working farms with on-site restaurants, wine produced on the property, and breakfast included. No grocery shopping ever. Dinner is on the farm.

1 Fattoria di Rignana — Greve in Chianti ~€102–168/night · Best value · Pool + wine + views ▼

Leading pick. A working wine and olive oil estate in the hills between Greve and Panzano in Chianti. Stone farmhouse apartments with vineyard views, outdoor pool, on-site restaurant serving estate wines and farm-to-table Tuscan dishes. Surrounded by the Chianti Classico DOCG zone — wine tastings on-site. Incredibly good value for what you get.

Why it works for you: Full meal service (breakfast + dinner available on the farm), their own Chianti Classico, pool, free parking, and at €102–168/night it leaves budget for great dinners out.

Fattoria di Rignana
2 Castello di Fonterutoli ~€200–350/night · Medieval hamlet, private winery ▼

An entire medieval village converted into a luxury stay by the Mazzei family — one of Chianti's most celebrated winemaking dynasties. Your accommodation is inside a 700-year-old village; the restaurant uses produce from the estate's gardens and olive groves, paired with the estate's own Chianti Classico. Private cellar tours and wine tasting are included. The pool is heated and surrounded by vines.

Castello di Fonterutoli
3 Rosewood Castiglion del Bosco ~$600–900/night · Ultra-luxury, Montalcino ▼

If budget is flexible, this is one of the finest properties in all of Italy — a 5,000-acre private estate near Montalcino, home to Brunello di Montalcino (arguably Italy's greatest red wine). The estate has its own vineyard, chapel, golf course, two restaurants, spa, tennis courts, and 39 private accommodations in restored stone farmhouses. This is the place for a truly exceptional stay. A 30-minute detour from Chianti but worth the drive.

Rosewood Castiglion del Bosco

Venice — 3 nights

Nov 1–4. Early November is magical: fewer tourists, mist on the lagoon, no summer heat, the light is extraordinary. No cars exist in Venice — all transport is by foot and vaporetto (water bus). Park concerns are gone.

Getting from train station to hotel: Arrive at Venezia Santa Lucia (on the island). Most hotels send a private water taxi for guests — arrange in advance. Or take the public vaporetto (Line 1 along the Grand Canal, €9.50 single or €25 for 48-hr pass per person).

What to do in Venice

Day 11 — Saturday Nov 1 · Arrive + Grand Canal

Check in. Walk the Rialto Bridge and Rialto Market (closes by noon — if arriving before lunch, go straight there). Vaporetto ride down the Grand Canal from the train station to San Marco (€9.50) — the best way to see the palaces.

Dinner: Osteria alle Testiere — 22-seat Venetian seafood osteria, one of the best restaurants in Italy. Book 3–4 weeks ahead. Lagoon cuttlefish, spider crab, Soave wine. The chef Marco buys directly from the fish market each morning.

Book Alle Testiere
Day 12 — Sunday Nov 2 · St. Mark's + Doge's Palace

Morning: St. Mark's Basilica — free, but book timed entry at veneziaunica.it (€3 reservation, avoids the 45-min queue). Climb the Campanile (bell tower) for rooftop lagoon views.

Doge's Palace — the political and artistic heart of Venice for 1,000 years. Book the Secret Itineraries tour (in hidden rooms including the prisons; this is where Casanova escaped from). Book at palazzoducale.visitmuve.it.

Afternoon: Get lost. Venice's best experience is wandering away from the tourist path into Dorsoduro or Cannaregio — small bridges, hidden campi (squares), local bars serving cicchetti (Venetian tapas) for €1–2 each.

Dinner: Trattoria da Marisa (Cannaregio, zero tourists, fixed-menu Venetian food, cash only, reservations by phone) or Antiche Carampane (outstanding seafood, no pizza on menu — that's a good sign).

Day 13 — Monday Nov 3 · Murano + Burano

Take the vaporetto to the outer islands. Murano (glass-blowing capital of the world — watch a live demonstration at a real furnace, not a tourist shop) and Burano (colored fishing houses, the most photogenic neighborhood in Italy, famous for lace-making). November light on Burano's painted facades is otherworldly.

Last dinner in Venice: Venissa on Mazzorbo island (between Murano and Burano) — a Michelin-starred restaurant on a small vineyard island, lagoon views, extraordinary. Book well in advance for Nov 3.

Book Venissa

Where to stay in Venice — 3 options

1 The Gritti Palace (Marriott Luxury) ~€800–1,000/night · Grand Canal views ▼

Best value-to-luxury ratio for Venice. A restored 15th-century Doge's palace directly on the Grand Canal in San Marco. The terrace restaurant overlooks the water — one of the most beautiful dining experiences in Italy. Spa with treatments, private boat service, and the Hemingway Bar (he finished "Across the River" here). Three nights here for 3 people is a splurge but deeply worth it.

The Gritti Palace
2 Palazzo Venart Luxury Hotel ~€400–650/night · Garden, pool, quieter ▼

A restored 16th-century palace in Santa Croce with one of the only private gardens in Venice — and a pool (rare in a city built on water). 18 rooms, more intimate than the grand hotels, with outstanding breakfast. Their restaurant Glam has a Michelin star. Good for a quieter stay away from the San Marco crowds, 10-min walk to the Rialto.

Palazzo Venart
3 Hotel Danieli ~$1,500–2,000+/night · Most iconic · lagoon views ▼

Venice's most legendary hotel — a Gothic palazzo directly on the waterfront next to the Doge's Palace, with a rooftop terrace restaurant overlooking the entire lagoon and San Giorgio island. George Sand, Charles Dickens, and every head of state who visited Venice stayed here. Price is high but the rooftop views at sunset are unmatchable. If budget allows, worth one splurge night.

Hotel Danieli
